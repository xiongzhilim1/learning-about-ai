# What hiring managers and bookers look for in a portfolio

Research for issue #4 (child of map #2). Researched 2026-09-27.

Question: what do hiring managers for Deployed PM, Deployment Strategist / forward-deployed, and AI strategy roles look for in a candidate's portfolio or personal site, and what do speaking bookers and paid-project clients check?

## Method and source trust

- **Tier 1 (primary):** live job postings pulled from the companies' own applicant tracking systems (Anthropic via Greenhouse API, OpenAI via Ashby API, Palantir via Lever API), Anthropic's own careers and candidate guidance pages, a speakers bureau's own published listing criteria, and published buyer research (Hinge Research Institute, Ladders eye-tracking study).
- **Tier 2 (secondary, flagged inline):** event-planner vetting guides from speaker marketplaces, and portfolio-review posts by career mentors. Used only where they agree with Tier 1 or fill a gap Tier 1 does not cover.
- **Gap:** no company in scope publishes an explicit "what we look for in your personal site" statement for these roles. The portfolio signals below are therefore inferred from what the postings say the job *is* and how success is *measured*. Inference is marked as such.
- **Title note:** "Deployed PM" is not a current title at Anthropic, OpenAI or Palantir. The nearest live equivalents are OpenAI's *Deployment Lead (FDE)* and *AI Deployment Manager*, Palantir's *Deployment Strategist*, and Anthropic's *Applied AI Strategist* and *Applied AI Architect*.

## 1. AI lab and forward-deployed hiring managers

### What the postings say the job is (Tier 1)

**OpenAI, Deployment Lead (FDE), Singapore.** Success is "measured first and foremost by impact: deployments that deliver measurable value against customer goals, drive adoption, and become critical to their workflows," plus "delivery reliability," "operating leverage (patterns reused across deployments), judgment under pressure, and product impact (field signal that shifts roadmaps/architectures)." Responsibilities include "Codify solution patterns and evals" and "Own value cases and ROI. Set impact hypotheses, baselines, and KPIs; run pre-/post-deployment measurement and report to exec sponsors." Candidates should "Have shipped AI/LLM systems," be "a translator with executive presence," and "Have expertise in at least one major sector." ([posting](https://jobs.ashbyhq.com/openai/93568fc5-8c7a-4cc1-8aad-198d67bf348f))

**OpenAI, AI Deployment Manager (Builder).** A post-sales enablement role that "blends deep technical fluency, instructional design, and customer advisory." Asks for "Proven experience leading structured technical trainings, such as API bootcamps, workshops, or enablement sessions, with the ability to design learning journeys," "exceptional presentation and communication skills," and a "practical understanding of how AI systems are built, evaluated, and operated in production, including RAG, evaluation strategies, agentic systems and key tradeoffs." Also "Develop and refine reusable training assets, playbooks, and best practices." ([posting](https://jobs.ashbyhq.com/openai/9675cfd2-a983-49d7-80db-d3d58c42eb08))

**Anthropic, Applied AI Strategist, EMEA.** "This is not a hands-on engineering role." The job is to "walk into a boardroom," "help leaders see a compelling future centered on Claude," lead Executive Briefings, and "build the content, value frameworks and templates that let the wider team operate credibly at the executive level." Good fit signals: "Experience producing executive-grade content others reuse: industry perspectives, strategic proposals, success plans," "The ability to connect AI capabilities to strategic outcomes through clear value frameworks and business cases," and "Strong opinions about what makes enterprise transformation succeed or fail, earned by leading or advising these efforts." Requires 15+ years in enterprise tech or consulting at executive level. ([posting](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/anthropic/jobs/5390791008))

**Anthropic, Applied AI Architect (Sydney, representative of the APAC set).** "Create and deliver compelling technical content tailored to different audiences," "Help customers develop evaluation frameworks," "Identify common integration patterns and contribute insights back to our Product and Engineering teams," "A love of teaching, mentoring, and helping others succeed," "Comfortable with python." 10+ years in technical customer-facing roles. ([posting](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/anthropic/jobs/5117589008))

**Anthropic, Forward Deployed Engineer.** Wants "Production experience with LLMs including advanced prompt engineering, agent development, evaluation frameworks, and deployment at scale" and strong Python. This is the most engineering-heavy of the set. ([posting](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/anthropic/jobs/5302966008))

**Palantir, Deployment Strategist (Singapore).** "At its core, this role is about uncovering dots and, without knowing the shape they form, figuring out how to connect them." The mission is "to synthesize disconnected streams of thought into a cohesive understanding of what the most important problem is, what the data means, what the product needs, what users are motivated by, and where the impact could be." Day to day: go onsite to find pain points, build bespoke workflows, "Lead training sessions," "Present the results of our work and proposals for future work to audiences ranging from analysts to C-suite executives," "Build and deliver demos." Values people who "aren't satisfied with surface-level answers and will dive into the details of the data" and have "Extraordinary ability to take on open-ended problems in unstructured environments." Programming is "a plus," not required. ([posting](https://jobs.lever.co/palantir/e65e0d73-1d91-4696-809f-7810ecd474d1))

### What Anthropic says about evidence in general (Tier 1)

- "We care about what you can do, not where you learned to do it." and "If you've done interesting independent research, written a thoughtful blog post, or contributed to open source, put that at the top of your resume." For business roles: "clarity, judgment, and a genuine interest in the mission." ([Anthropic careers](https://www.anthropic.com/careers))
- "Use AI to refine your ideas, not replace them. We want to see your actual experience and how you think, not AI-generated responses." ([Anthropic candidate AI guidance](https://www.anthropic.com/candidate-ai-guidance))

### Signals a portfolio should carry (inferred from the above)

1. **Shipped deployments with measured outcomes.** OpenAI literally names the KPIs (impact hypothesis, baseline, pre/post measurement, adoption). A case study that follows that shape reads as "already doing the job."
2. **Reusable patterns, not one-offs.** "Operating leverage," "codify solution patterns and evals," "identify common integration patterns," "content others reuse." Playbooks, frameworks and templates that others have actually used are direct evidence.
3. **Evals literacy.** Evals appear in every Anthropic and OpenAI posting reviewed. Showing how you judged whether an AI system worked is a stronger signal than showing that you built one.
4. **Teaching and enablement.** Training, workshops, bootcamps and "learning journeys" are explicit requirements for OpenAI's AI Deployment Manager and appear in Palantir's and Anthropic's lists. Structured courses are on-target evidence here.
5. **Executive translation.** Every role asks for the ability to make technical trade-offs legible to C-suite. Writing and talks aimed at executives, not only at builders, demonstrate this.
6. **Sector depth and a point of view.** OpenAI asks for expertise in a major sector; Anthropic's strategist role asks for "strong opinions" earned by doing. Regional depth (for APAC, how large Greater China and ASEAN enterprises buy and adopt tech) is the analogue of Anthropic's EMEA framing.
7. **Authentic thinking.** Anthropic explicitly wants "how you think." Original frameworks and first-person reasoning beat polished generic prose.

## 2. Speaking bookers and event planners

### Bureau listing criteria (Tier 1, BigSpeak's own FAQ)

To be listed, BigSpeak says a speaker needs: "video footage of appearing on stage at several events/talks. A sizzle reel is preferred"; "testimonials from major companies"; to be "already earning a $10K+ range"; to be "working regularly, have inbound leads, and performed at least 10 paid gigs in the last year"; and "a book to show their credibility." Exceptions exist for "an in-demand topic, diversity appeal, or 'rising star' potential." ([BigSpeak FAQ](https://www.bigspeak.com/about/keynote-speakers-faqs-for-speakers-bureaus/))

Implication: bureau representation is a later-stage goal. Early bookings come direct from event organisers, who check the items below.

### What planners check (Tier 2, speaker-marketplace planner guides)

- **Unedited stage footage.** "Request at least 10 minutes of unedited stage time. This reveals the speaker's true pacing and audience rapport." and "A captivating demo video is often the most deceptive tool in an event planner's arsenal." ([Speakers.com vetting guide](https://speakers.com/vetting-keynote-speakers-2026-guide-for-event-planners/))
- **References from past organisers**, including how Q&A went and whether the speaker was easy to coordinate with. (same source)
- **Public footprint check** for brand misalignment in recent news and social. (same source)
- **Customisation on the discovery call**: "If a speaker seems more focused on their own rider than your event's goals, they may be difficult to manage." (same source)
- **Virtual readiness**: home studio lighting, audio, platform fluency. (same source)
- Reviews and video used together to judge fit. ([eSpeakers](https://www.espeakers.com/how-to-use-speaker-reviews-and-videos-to-select-the-right-speaker/))

### Signals a speaker page should carry

A clear list of 2 to 4 talk topics with audience and takeaway, at least one full-length unedited talk (plus a short clip), organiser testimonials, a downloadable speaker kit (headshots, short and long bio), formats offered (keynote, workshop, virtual), and an ungated contact path.

## 3. Paid-project and consulting clients

### How buyers find and vet (Tier 1, Hinge Research Institute)

- Consulting buyers' top deciding factors are "Talented staff/team skills that can fill the gaps" and "Relevant experience and strong industry knowledge and subject matter expertise." Over 83% of consulting buyers use LinkedIn. Firms "viewed as highly relevant to clients' current issues are 82% more likely to be highly recommended." (Inside the Buyer's Brain, Consulting Edition, 2020, 362 buyers and 369 sellers; [Hinge](https://hingemarketing.com/blog/story/new-research-consulting-buyer-criteria-and-priorities-are-changing-how-why))
- Across professional services, buyers find a new provider mostly by referral (71%), then by searching online (11%), and increasingly judge expertise from online evidence: "specialization, expert content, thought leadership, case studies and client lists." ([Hinge](https://hingemarketing.com/blog/story/new-research-study-shows-significant-changes-in-professional-services-buyer-behavior))

Implication: the site is mainly a **verification** surface for someone who was referred, not a discovery engine. It must confirm relevance to the buyer's current problem fast, through a clear specialisation, case studies and expert content.

## 4. How reviewers read (Tier 1 plus Tier 2)

- Recruiters spent an average of 7.4 seconds on an initial resume screen; simple layouts with clear headings did well, clutter, multiple columns and long sentences did poorly. (30 recruiters, 2018; [Ladders study PDF](https://www.theladders.com/static/images/basicSite/pdfs/TheLadders-EyeTracking-StudyC2.pdf)) This is resume data, but the same skim behaviour applies to a landing page.
- Tier 2 (design-portfolio mentor, not a hiring manager): reviewers decide whether to click, judge craft in seconds, scan the hero for fit, skim work for clarity, and only then read the About page. Generic hero statements are a named anti-pattern. ([Open Doors Careers](https://blog.opendoorscareers.com/p/how-recruiters-and-hiring-managers-actually-look-at-your-portfolio))

## Recommended proof formats

| Format | Serves | Why (source) |
|---|---|---|
| Deployment case study: problem, baseline, what shipped, eval method, measured outcome, reusable pattern | Hiring managers, clients | Mirrors OpenAI's success measures and Hinge's "case studies" |
| Frameworks and playbooks others reuse | Strategist roles, clients | Anthropic "content others reuse," OpenAI "operating leverage" |
| Structured courses and workshops | AI Deployment Manager, Applied AI, speaking | OpenAI enablement role, Palantir training, Anthropic "love of teaching" |
| Point-of-view essays on enterprise AI adoption in a region or sector | Strategist roles, clients, bookers | Anthropic "strong opinions," OpenAI sector expertise, Hinge "expert content" |
| Talk page with unedited footage, topics, testimonials, speaker kit | Bookers | BigSpeak criteria, Speakers.com vetting guide |
| Working demo or prototype with a short walkthrough | FDE-adjacent roles | Palantir "build and deliver demos," Anthropic Python/evals asks |

## Anti-patterns

1. **Artifacts without outcomes.** Showing *what* was built with no baseline, measurement or adoption story, when every deployment posting measures impact.
2. **Showcase over substance.** A striking visual experience that does not lead quickly to evidence of judgment. Reviewers skim; the hero must state fit.
3. **Generic or AI-sounding prose.** Anthropic asks to see "how you think," not AI-generated text.
4. **Builder-only framing for strategy roles, or strategy-only framing for FDE roles.** The postings split clearly: Anthropic's strategist role is "not a hands-on engineering role," while its FDE role needs production Python. Pick the target and lead with the matching evidence.
5. **No executive-facing work.** Every role asks for C-suite translation; a portfolio of only technical tutorials misses it.
6. **Polished sizzle reel only** for speaking. Planners want unedited stage time and organiser references.
7. **Gated or hidden contact paths** (Tier 2: planners abandon gated demos).
8. **Claims you cannot share.** Inference, not sourced: for someone at a large platform company, client names and numbers may be confidential. Anonymised or pattern-level case studies, plus independent work, avoid this.

## Implications for this site (inference)

- The three courses map directly to OpenAI's "AI Deployment Manager" and Anthropic's "love of teaching" and "content others reuse." Frame them as enablement assets with learning outcomes, not just content.
- The Digital Brain is a strong "how I think" artifact, but should hand off quickly to written case studies and frameworks.
- Missing pieces for the target audiences: deployment-style case studies with measured outcomes, an evals angle, executive-facing point-of-view writing on APAC enterprise AI adoption, and a speaking page with unedited footage and testimonials.
